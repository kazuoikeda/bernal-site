  • Paying members make up only about 1% of all users, yet they account for over 40% of all search usage.
  • Paying members use search about 57x as often as non-members (about 7% of paying members use search).
  • Where the search box sits changes usage frequency by about 130x (top pages vs article pages). On top pages, about 17% of paying members (1 in 6) use search, against about 1% of non-members.
  • Article pages account for over 90% of all page views, yet generate only around 10% of all search activity. We believe the placement difference drives the gap: below the banner on top pages, versus at the bottom of the article on article pages.
  • After we added the search box to top pages too, everyday searches unrelated to any paid article (news, sports scores and the like) became more common (a qualitative observation).

The Problem

A shared challenge for subscription media

Archives that never become assets

Paying members search over 50x as often as non-members, yet the search box sits in an easy-to-miss spot on article pages — so archives rarely become an entry point.

Ever-rising acquisition costs

The cost of acquiring new subscribers keeps climbing, while improving retention among existing members is too often left for later.

"I couldn't find it" as a reason to cancel

Not being able to find the article you were after is a small but reliable reason members let their subscription lapse.

The Solution

Bernal turns search into management data, along two axes

Intent-based search

Rather than matching keywords, we interpret what the reader is actually looking for — a design built to cut zero-result searches at the root.

Measurement by membership tier

We measure whether the person searching is a paying member, and make visible how search contributes to paywall conversion, billing and retention.

About us

Why Bernal

It isn't a technical edge. What you choose to measure, and who reads the result, is where the real difference lies.

Different buyers need different units of measurement

We break results down by paying member, free member and non-member. Most search tools don't. That isn't a lack of technical ability — it's that the buyer is usually a web team. Only once you measure at this level does a difference like that 57x figure come into view.

We read the logs ourselves

We read every zero-result search, one by one. Our reports aren't auto-generated — which is exactly why we can't take on many publishers at once.

We don't call an inconclusive number conclusive

Before we start, we agree in writing on what success looks like, and on what would count as "no effect". We don't move the goalposts once work is under way.

This method was filed for patent in 2017, and has since been granted in both Japan and the United States (Japanese Patent No. 6832584; US Patent No. 11,113,297; inventor: Kazuo Ikeda). We first applied it to advertising, without success. It was only once we applied it to subscription-business metrics that a meaningful difference emerged.
