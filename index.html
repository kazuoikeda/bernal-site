  • Paying members make up only about 1% of all users, yet they account for more than 40% of all searches.

Why Bernal

It isn't about technology

What you measure, and who reads the result, is where the difference lies.

We split results by membership type

We show results for paying members, free members and non-members separately. The people who decide to adopt Bernal are the team running the site. Only by splitting results this way did the 57-fold gap come to light.

A person reads every one

We read the zero-result searches ourselves, one by one. That is why we cannot take on many partners at once.

Before we start, we agree in writing what counts as ‘success’ and what counts as ‘no effect’. We don’t move the goalposts once work has begun.

Our view

Where we see this going

A search term is a small signal from a reader. Bernal reads these signals every day and shows media organisations what to do next.

A reader’s search term

Every day, without a break.

Shows the next move

Media can see what to do next.

We call the part of Bernal that does this a daemon. In software, a daemon is a process that runs quietly in the background, with no screen of its own — not something you open and close, but something that stays resident and keeps working, unprompted. That is the model we are building toward: not a report you request once a quarter, but a presence that lives inside your search logs and surfaces what matters the moment a pattern forms.

Today, that mostly means two things: pulling buried archive content back into view, and measuring how a reader’s search moves them toward a subscription. But a search box sees more than archive traffic. It sees what a headline failed to explain, what a push notification did not answer, what a reader could not find anywhere else on the site. As we read more of that, the daemon’s job grows — from a single number on a dashboard to a standing signal that reaches editorial, product and subscription teams at the moment it matters, not at the end of the month.

The goal is not a smarter search box. It is a media organisation that always knows what its own readers are asking for — because something is always listening.
