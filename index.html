  • Paying members make up only about 1% of all users, yet they account for more than 40% of all searches.
  • Paying members use search about 57 times as often as non-members (around 7% of paying members use it).
  • Where the search box sits makes about a 130-fold difference to how often it is used (top pages against article pages). On top pages, around 17% of paying members (1 in 6) use search, compared with about 1% of non-members.

The Problem

A shared challenge for subscription media

Past articles that go unused

Paying members use search far more than non-members. But on article pages, the search box is easy to miss.

The rising cost of winning members

Winning new members costs more every year, while helping current members to stay is often left until later.

‘I couldn’t find it’ as a reason to cancel

Not finding the article you wanted is a reason people stop renewing.

Why Bernal

We show results for paying members, free members and non-members separately. The people who decide to adopt Bernal are the team running the site. Only by splitting results this way did the 57-fold gap come to light.

What we are building

Bernal is building a layer that lives inside a publisher’s search logs and does two things: it puts buried archive content back in front of readers, matched to what they actually search for, and it measures how a reader’s search leads toward a subscription.

Internally, we call this layer the daemon: a process with no screen of its own that stays resident in the background and keeps working without being asked. We are building toward a system that does not wait for a quarterly report, but tells a team the moment a pattern changes.

It is designed to keep evolving. Like a living system, it changes every day to fit what its partners need, lighting the way one step ahead from behind the scenes.

Where it leads

A search box is one of the few places where readers tell a publisher, in their own words, what they want. It shows what a headline failed to explain, what a push notification did not answer, and what a reader could not find anywhere on the site. The more of that we learn to read, the more the daemon can give editorial, product and subscription teams a standing signal at the moment it matters, not at the end of the month.

The goal is not a better search box. It is a media organisation that always knows what its readers are asking for, because something is always listening.
