Policy on the Handling of User Information in the Bernal Search Service

Last updated: November 2026

Bernal, Inc. (the "Company") sets out this Policy on the Handling of User Information in the Bernal Search Service (this "Policy"), describing how we handle user information in connection with "Bernal" (the "Service"), the search intelligence we provide.

1. Company information

NameBernal, Inc.
RepresentativeKazuo Ikeda
ContactPlease see our Contact page

2. Overview of the Service

The Service is a search engine installed via an embedded tag on a publisher's (client's) website. We operate the Service on the client's instruction, and information arising from its use is, as a rule, processed self-containedly within our own systems (we do not re-share it with any third party).

However, when a search returns no results, the search terms are sent to the AI provider named in Section 8 and processed there, so that we can suggest related articles (by identifying the topic and through a short conversation). If the user replies in that conversation, the conversation is sent as well.

3. Information we collect

In providing the Service, the information we collect is as follows.

Search termsWords or phrases a user enters into the Service's search box
Anonymous IDA randomly generated identifier stored in the browser's localStorage (contains no name, membership details or other identifying information)
Session IDA randomly generated identifier stored in the browser's sessionStorage (cleared when the tab is closed)
Per-search IDA random identifier generated in the browser each time a search is run (used to link the results shown, page moves, clicks and similar events of the same search; not stored in the browser)
Per-page-view IDA random identifier generated in the browser each time a page is viewed (used to link that page view with the page-reading information below; not stored in the browser)
Page URLThe URL of the page on which the search box is installed, and the path of the canonical URL stated on the page (without query strings or similar)
Usage event dataPage views; display of, focus on and typing into the search box; search executions; the number of results shown and moves between result pages; articles clicked and their position; display and selection of popular search terms and suggestions; what is shown and done when a search returns no results; clicks on guide links; display of and clicks on related articles; how long the search box stayed in focus and whether anything was typed (the text typed is not collected under this item); the articles shown in search results and their position; where the search box is placed; device type (mobile, tablet or desktop only); the group assigned in comparison tests of search features; and timestamps
Page-reading informationVisible time on the page (while the tab is shown and the user is active); maximum scroll depth; how the page was opened (normal navigation, reload or back); a category of page load speed; a category of screen size and orientation (exact screen dimensions are not collected); and actions within an article (clicks on links to related or next articles, and clicks on external links, for which only the destination host name is collected)
Referral categoryOnly a broad category of what the user was viewing before arriving at the page (such as a search engine, social media, the same site, another external site, or direct). The conversion to a category happens in the browser; we do not collect the full referring URL, its path, search keywords or host name
Membership categoryOnly where provided by the client: whether the user is a paying member, a free member or not logged in (no member ID, name or similar information)
Conversation contentIn the conversation shown when a search returns no results, the text a user enters and the Service's replies. This is processed only to produce the replies and is not stored in our database

IP address and browser information (User-Agent) reach our servers as an inherent feature of how data is transmitted over the internet. Of these, the only thing we store in our search logs is the device type derived from the browser information (mobile, tablet or desktop); we do not store the browser information itself. We do not store IP addresses in our search logs. To limit traffic and prevent unauthorised access, we may temporarily hold a value derived from the IP address.

The anonymous ID, session ID, per-search ID and per-page-view ID above are all randomly generated identifiers. We do not link them to a name, email address or any other information capable of identifying an individual.

Our system is technically capable of linking search logs to a client's own member IDs, but we do not currently use this capability to store data in that way. Should a client implement this in future, this would no longer apply, and we would notify the client concerned individually.

4. How we collect it

Information is collected automatically, in the user's browser, through the Service's programme (tag), when a user views or reads a page on which the Service's tag is installed and when a user interacts with the search box.

5. Purpose of use

  1. To provide the Service (search functionality)
  2. To improve search accuracy
  3. To produce usage reports for clients (such as popular keywords and click-through rates)
  4. To suggest related articles to users whose search returned no results
  5. To run comparison tests (A/B tests) to check how easy the search features are to use
  6. To aggregate terms searched by at least a certain number of users and show them as popular search terms or suggestions (terms searched by only a few users, or that could identify an individual, are not shown)
  7. To measure how articles are read (such as read-through rate and time spent), separating out factors outside the article such as where readers came from, and to use this in reports for clients and to improve search results
  8. To correct for biases in how search results are shown and clicked, so that articles that have not yet been read are easier to find

We do not use this information beyond the purposes above.

6. External transmission (Article 27-12 of the Telecommunications Business Act)

When a user views or interacts with a page on which the Service's tag is installed, the following information is sent to our servers (the recipient).

Information transmittedSearch terms, anonymous ID, session ID, per-search ID, per-page-view ID, page URL (including the canonical URL path), membership category (only where provided by the client), usage event data (page views, display of/focus on/typing into the search box, time in focus, search executions, number of results shown, moves between result pages, clicks, ranking, articles shown in results, display and selection of popular terms and suggestions, what is shown and done when there are no results, placement, device type, comparison-test group, timestamps, etc.), page-reading information (visible time, maximum scroll depth, how the page was opened, load-speed category, screen size and orientation category, actions within an article), and referral category
Name of recipientBernal, Inc.
Purpose of transmissionTo provide the Service (search functionality), to improve search accuracy, to produce usage reports for clients (including how articles are read), and to run comparison tests of search features

We do not link the information transmitted to anything beyond what is described in Section 3.

7. Retention period

Raw data: we have not currently set a retention period, and do not carry out deletion.
Aggregated data: we do not store aggregated data separately; it is calculated afresh from the raw data each time it is shown in our dashboard.

We plan to keep raw data for 90 days from collection, after which it will be replaced with daily, per-article aggregates that cannot identify an individual, and the raw data deleted. We will update this section when this is in place.

8. Disclosure to third parties

Except where required by law, we do not disclose the information we collect to any third party.

However, we entrust the processing behind article suggestions for searches that return no results (topic identification and the conversation) to an AI provider based in the United States. We send them only the search terms that returned no results, the text a user enters in the conversation, and the titles, opening lines and categories of the client's articles that are candidates for suggestion. We do not send the anonymous ID, session ID, IP address, page URL or member ID. Our contract with this provider prohibits any use beyond the entrusted purpose, including use for training AI models.

We do use an external vendor for the development and maintenance of the systems behind the Service; the safeguards covering that arrangement are set out in Section 10, under "Technical safeguards."

9. About cookies

The Service (search widget): The Service does not itself use cookies or any similar device identifier, and none are sent.

This website (bernal-inc.com): Our corporate website uses Google Analytics, an analytics tool provided by Google, to understand how the site is used and to help us improve it. Google Analytics collects information via cookies, but this does not include information capable of identifying you by name. This use of cookies is enabled only if you consent via the banner shown when you visit the site; if you do not consent, no measurement takes place. Information collected is managed by Google in accordance with Google's own Privacy Policy. For details of Google Analytics' terms and privacy practices, please see Google's Privacy Policy. You can also opt out of measurement using the Google Analytics Opt-out Browser Add-on.

10. Security measures

To prevent the leakage, loss or damage of the information we handle, and to maintain its security more generally, we take the following measures.

Organisational safeguardsWe have established a policy for handling data (this Policy), and have put in place in advance a reporting and escalation process for when a data breach occurs or is suspected.
Personnel safeguardsAccess to data is limited to those who need it for their work. As our team grows, any expansion of who may access data happens only after we have agreed confidentiality undertakings with, and provided data-handling training to, the individuals concerned.
Physical safeguardsData relating to the Service is managed in data centres in Japan (Tokyo) provided by cloud providers, and we also control access to the work devices we use, to prevent unauthorised viewing by third parties.
Technical safeguardsData is encrypted both at rest and in transit, and we apply access controls such as IDs and passwords, together with measures to prevent unauthorised access. Where we sub-contract the development or maintenance of our systems, we enter into an agreement with the vendor imposing confidentiality obligations and prohibiting use beyond the agreed purpose, and we structure the work so the vendor has no direct access to production data.

11. Requests for disclosure, deletion and similar

Information collected through the Service is recorded under an anonymous ID that contains no name, email address or similar details, so we are normally unable to identify a particular person's information. For requests concerning disclosure, correction, suspension of use or deletion, please contact the company that has installed the Service on its website, or reach us via our Contact page. If you can provide the anonymous ID stored in your browser, we will delete the information recorded under that ID (information already aggregated into a form that cannot identify an individual is excluded).

12. Changes to this Policy

This Policy may be changed without notice, to reflect changes in the law or other circumstances. Any change takes effect from the moment it is published on this page.

13. Enquiries

For enquiries about this Policy, please contact us via our Contact page.
